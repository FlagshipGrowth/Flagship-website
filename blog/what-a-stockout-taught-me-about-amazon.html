I Learned More From One Product Going Viral Than From Any Campaign I've Run

Joey Giazzon·October 27, 2026·3 min read

I learned more from one product going viral than from any campaign I have ever run.

Before the agency, I built and ran my own Amazon brand. One of those products, BB-Bands, ended up being used by Team USA Baseball.

That should have been the win. What it actually was, was the hardest lesson I have had in this business.

Demand showed up faster than I could supply it. I had the traffic, the credibility, and the kind of proof most brands never get.

And I stocked out.

Rank slid while I waited on inventory. Competitors picked up the keywords I had earned. When stock landed, I was not buying back attention. I was buying back position, which is far more expensive.

Two things that stuck with me permanently

The first is that operations are marketing. The best campaign in the world is a liability if the supply chain behind it cannot keep a promise. I now care about a client's lead times as much as their ACoS, and clients are always surprised by that.

The second is that being the owner changes what you notice. I felt every stockout in my own bank account. Later, agency-side, I watched people manage other brands' accounts without ever having felt that.

That gap is most of why I built Flagship the way I did.

You cannot fake having lost your own money. It changes which risks you flag and how early you flag them.

The viral moment is not the achievement. Being ready for it is.

What would break in your business tomorrow if demand tripled?
